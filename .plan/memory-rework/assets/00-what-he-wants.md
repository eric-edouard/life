## What he wants

One sentence: an observer that has read his whole life and tells him, on the first try and with evidence, what
happened, where it stands, what keeps repeating and what comes next, about him, about each person and about each part
of his life.

As a grid: four questions, three subjects.

| | What happened | Where it stands | What keeps repeating | What comes next |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A person, and him with them | | | | |
| A part of his life (area, project, goal) | | | | |
| Him | | | | |

His seven areas sit on it: practical is the first column; areas and projects, goals and vision are the second row
(vision: where the years are heading against what he says he wants); relationships are the first row; psych and meta
are the third.

What makes an answer right, from his words:

1. **Everything, every year.** Every source, back to the oldest. What matters never lives only in the raw data.
2. **Direct.** A ready answer of the kind asked, on the first try, with no steering: a question about a relationship
   gets the relationship, never the logistics.
3. **What was done, not only what was said.** Between the lines of an exchange: who yields, blames, apologises, goes
   silent, deletes; his part, always.
4. **Over time.** How a relationship, a project, a period moved: turning points, what built up, what changed.
5. **The observer.** Life tells him things about himself he never told it, from his actions set against his words:
   where his time and money go against his goals, what he says against what he does, what repeats in him across people
   and years, how he felt over time, which promises are kept, both ways.
6. **Forecasts that earned trust**, about people and about him, scored against what really followed before they
   drive anything.
7. **Evidence on every line** (a quote, a count, a date); "not enough to say" is an answer.

Around it, already decided (map): his word beats any reading and survives every re-read; the practical answers stay
as good; more detail near now, broader with age; lean; built to outlive a model change; every feature of today stays.

Readings to confirm with him: "meta" is (a) what Life sees about him that he doesn't say and (b) the big picture
across areas, not Life describing itself. "Knows everything" means it can answer, not that every sentence is copied
into a layer.

The working definition of done: his real questions (about 400, each with what a right answer holds) answered on the
first try from memory.

## What worked

- Raw kept forever, the archive of deleted messages, units cut by code with ids that never move.
- "What happened and what is owed": episodes, quote-checked facts, open items, appointments, cited pages. It is most
  of what he asks day to day.
- His word beats a reading; fact checks answered in a chat; Life runs itself.
- Search by words and by meaning.
- The hand pilot on two people: the raw data does hold the patterns, and a strong model reading a whole raw thread
  finds them. The capability is in the model; the memory doesn't keep what it finds.

## What didn't, by cause

1. **The memory keeps what was said, never what was done.** A fact is a claim. Conduct in an exchange, feelings
   (the episode prompt forbids them), how a promise ended, where his time went: recorded nowhere, or stored and
   never counted. (Mail causes 1 and 3; #390 items 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9; problem 4.3.)
2. **Nothing holds time.** Each overview reads a window (a day, 30 days, a token budget); nothing rolls up into a
   period or an arc; the memory starts in Sep 2026. (Problem 4.1, 4.2; #390 items 3, 10, 11; mail cause 2.)
3. **"Where things stand" exists for some people only, as a status.** None for an area, a goal, his vision, him over
   time; and volume decides who gets one. (Problem 4.5; ticket 01's gaps.)
4. **Psychology is prose from one pass.** Not counted, not stable, not checkable; extremes averaged to the middle,
   demands read as traits, his performed words read as his view. No forecast exists. (Problem 4.4 and 5.)
5. **The plumbing punishes improvement.** Several layers read the same raw data and can disagree; a re-read changes
   fact ids and orphans his ratings; stale open items never retire; some loops nobody owns. (Problem 4.6 to 4.8; his
   practical fails.)
6. **Agents are sent to the practical page.** "How is this person" returns logistics, capped at the latest items; his
   own "stay practical" preference pushes further. (Mail causes 6 and 7.)

## What the psychology research adds

- One method under all of it: store quoted acts per exchange as evidence; count meters and patterns from them by
  code; never store a model's verdict about a person.
- Reading a trait straight from text is weak (about a stranger's accuracy). Counting many coded exchanges is the
  honest route, with "too few to say" as a state.
- Focused reads, one family of codes each, beat one wide read. Stronger models code better.
- Time first: a pattern can be true for two years and faded since.
- Proof is prediction: hold out what followed, predict it, score against base rates. Forecasts start from base rates;
  a model adjusts only where backtests show it helps.
- Refused: types and diagnoses as stored facts (MBTI only later, computed, as he decided).

## How it fits together

The mail, the issue and the research say the same thing from three sides. Between the units and the views, Life
needs one layer of evidence about behaviour: what each person, him included, said and did, exchange by exchange and
day by day, quoted, timed and counted, light on interpretation. His 10 Nov point is the same: the first layer above
units extracts as much as the raw holds and outlives a model change.

Everything he wants is then a count or a reading over that layer: a meter, a pattern, a promise's track record, time
against goals, an arc, where a thing stands, a forecast. Counts are free and stable; readings are cheap to rebuild.
